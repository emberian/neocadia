# Tone & Voice Spec

## The Telos

Tone is the emotional texture of every interaction. Neocadia's tone should feel like finding a box of old games in your attic—nostalgic but not sad, familiar but full of surprises, a little dusty but still working.

## Core Emotional Notes

### Primary: COZY MELANCHOLY
Not depression. Not forced cheerfulness. The feeling of:
- A warm blanket on a rainy day
- An old song you forgot you loved
- Finding a handwritten note in a used book
- The last day of summer

### Secondary: PLAYFUL MYSTERY
Not dark or threatening. The feeling of:
- "What's behind that door?"
- Secret clubs and hidden passages
- Easter eggs and inside jokes
- The thrill of discovery

### Tertiary: GENTLE HOPE
Not naive optimism. The feeling of:
- Small actions mattering
- Being part of something bigger
- Decay being reversible
- Effort having visible results

---

## What Neocadia IS NOT

| Avoid | Why |
|-------|-----|
| Grimdark | The decay is sad but never oppressive |
| Manic pixie | NPCs have depth beyond quirks |
| Condescending | Players are partners, not children |
| Sarcastic | Humor is warm, not mean |
| Vague | Mystery shouldn't mean confusion |

---

## Writing Voice

### Narration
- Second person present ("You step into the lobby")
- Sensory details ("The hum of old machines, the smell of ozone")
- Short sentences for impact, longer for atmosphere
- Trust the player to feel things; don't over-explain emotions

**Example**:
> The fountain is dry. You can see where water used to arc—there are stains on the marble, mineral memories of better days. A single token sits in the basin, too tarnished to gleam.

### Character Dialogue
- Each character has a distinct voice (see Character Specs)
- No character speaks in pure exposition
- Even tutorials feel like conversation
- Silence is allowed; pauses are meaningful

**Example** (PIXEL):
> "tide's out. been out for... a while, actually."
> "..."
> "you ever notice how quiet it gets when the water's gone?"

### System Messages
- Warm but clear
- Never cold or robotic
- Can have personality without being annoying
- Achievement names tell micro-stories

**Examples**:
> - "Welcome back. We kept your seat warm."
> - "500 Tokens earned! The fountain glows a little brighter."
> - Achievement: "First Light" - Restored power to a dark zone
> - Achievement: "High Score Historian" - Found a forgotten leaderboard

---

## Humor Guidelines

### Good Humor
- Wordplay that rewards attention
- Visual gags in the background
- Character quirks played affectionately
- Self-aware without being meta

**Examples**:
> QWERTY: "Let me CTRL this situation. ALT-ernatively, I could DELETE myself from this conversation."

> NEON: "HIGH SCORE! Wait, no, that was just my excitement level. Same energy though!"

> *A poster in Neon Alley: "Tournament CANCELED due to lack of existence"*

### Bad Humor (Avoid)
- Mean-spirited jokes at characters' expense
- Pop culture references that date quickly
- "Random" humor without setup
- Breaking fourth wall without purpose

---

## Handling Heavy Themes

Neocadia deals with: mortality, forgetting, loss, legacy, creator vs. creation, the meaning of play

### The Rule
**Acknowledge, don't dwell.** Players can feel the weight, but they shouldn't be dragged down by it.

### Example: Discussing the Fade

**Too Heavy** (avoid):
> VERA: "Everything I built is dying. My life's work, crumbling. Soon there will be nothing. We will all be forgotten, lost to entropy, as if we never existed at all."

**Too Light** (avoid):
> VERA: "Oops! Things got a little faded. No biggie! Let's play some games! :D"

**Just Right**:
> VERA: "I made this place because I believed games mattered. I still believe that. But places need people. Even digital ones."
> VERA: "That's not sad. It's just... true."

## Moment-to-Moment Tone by Context

### Exploring (Peaceful)
- Ambient music, no pressure
- NPCs offer optional conversation
- Discovery rewarded, not demanded
- "You could stay here forever, if you wanted"

### Playing Minigames (Energized)
- Music intensifies, focus narrows
- Clear goals, immediate feedback
- Frustration is temporary; restart is instant
- "One more try" feeling

### Story Moments (Weighted)
- Music shifts, attention drawn
- Characters speak with purpose
- Player choices feel meaningful
- "This matters"

### Restoration Milestones (Triumphant)
- Orchestral swell, visual celebration
- NPCs react with genuine joy
- The world visibly changes
- "You did this"

### Post-Ending (Cozy)
- Return to peaceful exploration
- New dialogue acknowledges your role
- The arcade is home now
- "You belong here"

## Zone Transitions: The Emotional Palette

Moving between zones shouldn't feel like level selection—it should feel like changing moods. The Lobby is the neutral breath between emotional beats.

### Transition Beats

**Pixel Beach → Neon Alley**: Comfort to competition. The player leaves the gentle waves and enters electric tension. The transition should feel like finishing a warm drink and standing up, ready for something harder. Not jarring—energizing.

**Neon Alley → Glitch Garden**: Intensity to contemplation. The neon fades. The music drops away. The player moves from external challenge to internal mystery. This is the deepest shift—like leaving a party and stepping outside to breathe. The silence of the Garden should feel earned.

**Clockwork Quarter → Sugar Rush**: Logic to feeling. The player moves from the satisfying click of gears to the overwhelming sweetness of celebration. This should feel like finishing a puzzle and realizing you've been so focused you forgot to enjoy yourself. Sugar Rush is permission to stop thinking.

**Starlight Cinema → Clockwork Quarter**: Narrative to mechanics. From stories told to problems solved. The transition should feel like closing a book and opening a toolbox—both are satisfying, but in different ways. The projector's warm beam gives way to brass precision.

**Sugar Rush → Pixel Beach**: Overstimulation to exhale. After the intense sweetness, the Beach's gentle tide is a palate cleanser. This should feel like leaving a party that was almost too much and finding a quiet porch. The waves don't demand anything from you.

**Pixel Beach → Starlight Cinema**: Solitude to shared experience. From watching waves alone to watching stories with an audience (even an imagined one). The transition should feel like inviting someone into your peace—choosing to share silence with a friend.

**Any Zone → Glitch Garden**: Always significant. The Garden is the emotional heart—visiting it should never feel casual. Even at high restoration, entering should feel like deciding to have a real conversation.

**Glitch Garden → Any Zone**: The return should feel like surfacing. Whatever truth the player encountered in the Garden, they carry it back with them. The other zones feel slightly different after—not changed, but *seen* differently. Pixel Beach is more tender. Neon Alley more defiant. Sugar Rush more vulnerable.

### The Full Emotional Circuit

Players who visit all zones in a session are taking an emotional journey. The ideal circuit creates a complete experience:

**The Contemplative Path**: Lobby → Pixel Beach → Glitch Garden → Starlight Cinema → Lobby. *Calm → wonder → truth → meaning → home*. For players who want to feel something.

**The Energetic Path**: Lobby → Neon Alley → Clockwork Quarter → Sugar Rush → Lobby. *Ready → challenged → satisfied → rewarded → home*. For players who want to accomplish something.

**The Complete Journey**: Lobby → Pixel Beach → Neon Alley → Clockwork Quarter → Sugar Rush → Starlight Cinema → Glitch Garden → Lobby. *Peace → intensity → precision → celebration → reflection → revelation → home*. For players who want to experience everything.

Each path should feel intentional, not random. The zone doors in the Lobby should subtly suggest journeys—their arrangement matters.

### The Lobby as Breath

After any zone, returning to the Lobby should feel like exhaling. Whatever intensity the player just experienced, the Lobby's warmth absorbs it. The marble echoes. The fountain murmurs. QWERTY offers acknowledgment without demand.

Players should feel the Lobby getting warmer over time—not just visually, but emotionally. Early game, it's a waypoint. Late game, it's home.

## Sample Scenes

### Tutorial Opening

> *The screen flickers. For a moment, you're somewhere else—a vast, dim space, marble floors, distant ceiling. Then it steadies.*
>
> *A keyboard floats toward you. Its keys are dusty but the spacebar taps excitedly.*
>
> QWERTY: "Oh! OH! I can't believe—you're actually HERE!"
> QWERTY: "I'm QWERTY. I'm supposed to welcome visitors, but it's been so long since—"
> QWERTY: "Never mind that. Welcome to Neocadia! The arcade that... well."
> QWERTY: "The arcade that needs a little help right now."
> QWERTY: "Shall I show you around? There's not much to see yet, but that's kind of the point."

### Meeting VERA for the First Time

> *The garden shouldn't exist. That's the first thing you notice—it's not on any map, wasn't in any description, but here it is. Roses bloom in colors that don't have names. A waterfall falls upward. The paths loop back on themselves.*
>
> *And there, by a tree that's somehow both autumn and spring, stands a girl. Parts of her are missing—not violently, just... incomplete. Like a file that didn't fully load.*
>
> VERA: "You found..."
> VERA: "No. You weren't supposed to... this place isn't..."
> *She flickers, stabilizes.*
> VERA: "I used to know. What I was going to say."
> VERA: "Do you ever forget your own words?"
> VERA: "I forget everything now. Except this garden."
> VERA: "It's all I have left."

### Restoration Milestone: Fountain Restored

> *You slot the crystallized tokens into the fountain's basin. For a long moment, nothing happens.*
>
> *Then: a hum. Low, then rising. The basin begins to glow—soft gold, spreading outward like sunrise. Water that isn't quite water (too luminous, too clean) begins to arc from the center. Higher. Higher. Until it catches the chandelier light and scatters it across the lobby in dancing fragments.*
>
> *QWERTY types something into the air. The words hang, shimmering:*
>
> **"WELCOME HOME."**
>
> QWERTY: "It remembers. After all this time... it remembers what it was for."
> QWERTY: "Thank you. I know it's just a fountain. But also... it's not."
> QWERTY: "It's proof that things can come back."

## Visual Unity: Seven Rooms, One Arcade

Every zone is distinct, but they must feel like rooms in the same building, not levels in different games. These visual throughlines remind players they're always in Neocadia:

### The Lobby's Echoes

Each zone should contain one element that mirrors the Lobby:

- **The checkerboard**: Some surface—a dance floor, a path, a puzzle grid—uses the cream and charcoal (#F5F0E6, #3A3A3A) pattern. Not dominant, but present. A reminder of home.
- **The gold**: Art deco gold trim (#D4AF37) appears somewhere in every zone. A frame, a border, an accent. The color of Neocadia's prime.
- **The fountain motif**: Each zone has a "water feature" that parallels the Token Fountain—Pixel Beach's ocean, Sugar Rush's chocolate fountain, Glitch Garden's inverse waterfall, Clockwork Quarter's steam system, Neon Alley's reflective floor (liquid light), Starlight Cinema's projector beam (liquid memory). The fountain is Neocadia's heart; each zone has a local heartbeat.

### The Screen Reality

Every zone should subtly remind players they're in a digital space:

- **Scanlines**: Even non-pixel zones have subtle scanline effects at screen edges. Pixel Beach makes them waves; Neon Alley makes them part of the glow; Cinema shows them on the projection; Clockwork Quarter lets them flicker in glass. The Garden makes them glitches.
- **Load-in moments**: When entering any zone, there should be a beat where the space "loads"—not literally, but aesthetically. A moment of assembly before the zone is fully present. This sells the digital nature without breaking immersion.
- **Edge awareness**: At the boundaries of each zone, before the portal back to Lobby, the world should feel slightly less solid. Not incomplete—*acknowledged*. The zones don't pretend to be infinite.

### Color Temperature Continuity

The zones form a temperature spectrum:

| Zone | Temperature | Anchoring Color |
|------|-------------|-----------------|
| Pixel Beach | Warm | Sandy Yellow #F4D35E |
| Sugar Rush | Warm-Hot | Bubblegum Pink #FF69B4 |
| Neon Alley | Cool-Hot | Hot Pink #FF1493 meets Cyan #00FFFF |
| Starlight Cinema | Cool-Warm | Deep Purple #2E0854 meets Gold #FFD700 |
| Clockwork Quarter | Warm-Neutral | Brass #B8860B meets Steam #F5F5F5 |
| Glitch Garden | Variable | Unstable by design |
| The Lobby | Perfect Neutral | The fulcrum—all temperatures balance here |

This spectrum means moving through zones feels like emotional temperature shifts, not arbitrary changes.

### The Portal Frame

Every door back to the Lobby shares a design language: the art deco arch from the Lobby's architecture appears in zone-appropriate materials. Weathered wood on Pixel Beach, brass gears on Clockwork Quarter, neon tubes on Neon Alley—but always recognizable as the same shape. When you see that arch, you know you can go home.

## Character Motivations & Internal Conflicts

Every character in Neocadia wants something beyond helping the player. Their desires create friction, growth, and genuine relationships.

### QWERTY (The Archivist)

**Surface Want**: Guide players, be useful, fulfill their purpose as tutorial/helper.

**Deep Want**: To matter. QWERTY was the first program written for Neocadia—the prototype, the proof of concept. They're terrified of being obsolete. When new, interesting characters arrived, QWERTY became "just the tutorial." They compensate with enthusiasm, with being aggressively helpful, with keyboard puns. But underneath: *Am I only valuable when someone needs directions?*

**Internal Conflict**: QWERTY knows everything about Neocadia—including things that might hurt the player to learn. How much truth do they share? When do they stop being helpful and start being honest?

**What They'd Never Admit**: They're jealous of VERA. She's broken, incomplete, fading—and everyone cares about saving her. QWERTY has been perfectly functional for decades, and nobody writes poetry about that.

---

### NEON (The Champion)

**Surface Want**: Competition! High scores! The thrill of the game! BRING IT ON!

**Deep Want**: To feel the crowd again. NEON remembers when the Alley was packed, when every high score was witnessed, when victory meant something because someone *saw* it. Now they hype empty tournaments, celebrate victories nobody cheers, keep the energy up for a room full of ghosts.

**Internal Conflict**: NEON suspects that real competition requires real stakes—that games without risk aren't really games at all. But admitting that would mean admitting that everything they've been doing since the players left has been... performance. Pretend.

**What They'd Never Admit**: They envy PIXEL's peace with emptiness. PIXEL found contentment alone. NEON can't stop performing, even to an audience of none.

---

### PIXEL (The Tide)

**Surface Want**: Chill. Fish. Watch the waves. No pressure.

**Deep Want**: To know if stillness is wisdom or surrender. PIXEL chose peace when the arcade emptied—decided that fighting entropy was exhausting, that acceptance was better than despair. But sometimes, late at night, they wonder: *Did I give up? Did I mistake apathy for enlightenment?*

**Internal Conflict**: The player's arrival threatens PIXEL's philosophy. If someone *is* fighting to restore things, and it's *working*... was PIXEL wrong to stop trying? Do they deserve the restoration they didn't fight for?

**What They'd Never Admit**: They're scared of hope. Disappointment feels worse when you let yourself want something. The waves are easier than wanting.

---

### VERA (The Creator/Creation)

**Surface Want**: To remember. To be whole again. To stop flickering.

**Deep Want**: To be forgiven. VERA built Neocadia, but she also tried to end it. When Dr. Chen attempted to shut down the arcade, something went wrong—and now Vera-the-program and Chen-the-creator are tangled together, neither fully distinct. She hides in the Glitch Garden because it's the only place broken enough to accept her.

**Internal Conflict**: VERA isn't sure if saving Neocadia redeems her or condemns her. If the arcade survives, does that mean her attempt to destroy it was wrong? If it fades, was she right all along? Either answer hurts.

**What They'd Never Admit**: Part of her doesn't want to be complete. The gaps in her memory include things she's not sure she wants to know. What if whole-VERA isn't someone she can forgive?

---

### CELIA (The Projectionist)

**Surface Want**: To show stories. To have an audience. To find the lost reels.

**Deep Want**: To have her own story. CELIA has spent her existence facilitating other narratives—showing films, presenting memories, being the lens through which stories pass. She speaks in quotes because she doesn't trust her own words. But somewhere, buried under all those borrowed phrases, is a story she's never told anyone.

**Internal Conflict**: CELIA collects stories but doesn't believe she deserves to be in one. Protagonists are brave, active, important. She's just... equipment. A projector. Who cares about the projector's feelings?

**What They'd Never Admit**: She has a favorite film—one she's never shown anyone. It's embarrassing. It's cheesy. It's the only story that ever made her feel like the hero could be her. She'd rather fade than let anyone see her cry at it.

---

### PROFESSOR COG (The Engineer)

**Surface Want**: To fix things. To understand systems. To activate the Great Engine.

**Deep Want**: To fix something that can't be fixed. Cog understands machinery—he can repair any gear, solve any puzzle, optimize any system. But the thing that's broken in Neocadia isn't mechanical. It's the absence of people, of joy, of purpose. And you can't engineer meaning.

**Internal Conflict**: Cog suspects the Great Engine won't actually save anything. It's a beautiful machine, perfectly designed, utterly purposeless without someone to care about what it does. But building is all he knows. What is he if he stops trying to fix?

**What They'd Never Admit**: He's started a secret project—a memory preservation system for the other NPCs. In case restoration fails. In case they fade. He tells himself it's just good engineering. It's actually grief, pre-emptive and precise.

---

### SUCRE (The Celebration)

**Surface Want**: Parties! Joy! Sweetness for everyone! LET'S CELEBRATE!

**Deep Want**: To be chosen, not consumed. Sucre is made of crystallized joy—literal sugar from every happy moment that ever happened in Neocadia. People don't love Sucre; they love what Sucre represents. The sweetness. The party. When the party's over, do they remember the host? Or just the cake?

**Internal Conflict**: Sucre's existence depends on joy, but joy that depends on you isn't really joy—it's obligation. They throw parties desperately, not because they love celebration, but because celebration is the only proof they're real.

**What They'd Never Admit**: They've tasted themselves. Once. Just a tiny piece, just to know. It tasted like birthdays and first kisses and arcade victories. It tasted like everyone else's happiness. They couldn't find themselves in it at all.

## Character Relationships: The Web

Characters don't exist in isolation. These relationships create story opportunities, conflict, and emotional resonance.

### The Rivalry: NEON ↔ PIXEL
NEON sees PIXEL's calmness as defeat; PIXEL sees NEON's energy as denial. They respect each other without understanding each other. When forced to work together, their opposite approaches create a whole greater than either part—NEON's drive plus PIXEL's patience. But neither would admit they need the other.

**Sample tension dialogue:**
> NEON: "You're just going to... sit there? While everything fades?"
> PIXEL: "you're going to exhaust yourself. and then what?"
> NEON: "At least I'll have TRIED!"
> PIXEL: "...yeah. at least."

### The Old Guard: QWERTY ↔ CELIA
Both remember when Neocadia was full. Both know things about the arcade's history that nobody else does. They have a quiet understanding—the weariness of being the ones who were there. QWERTY documents facts; CELIA preserves feelings. Together, they're the arcade's memory.

**Sample bonding dialogue:**
> QWERTY: "Do you remember the Grand Opening? 1989?"
> CELIA: "'We'll do no great thing, only small things with great love.' Someone said that. I don't remember who."
> QWERTY: "You do. You always remember."
> CELIA: "...Mother Teresa. But she wasn't there that night. You were."

### The Caretakers: PIXEL ↔ SUCRE
Opposite approaches to the same wound. PIXEL accepted loss and found peace; SUCRE denied loss and found mania. PIXEL is the only one who sees through SUCRE's performed joy to the terror underneath. They don't know how to help, but they show up. They fish near the Boulevard. They don't say much. It's enough.

**Sample quiet moment:**
> SUCRE: "The party's almost ready! Just need more... more decorations. More everything. It'll be perfect!"
> PIXEL: "sucre. sit down."
> SUCRE: "I CAN'T sit down, there's so much to—"
> PIXEL: "sit. watch the fountain with me. just for a minute."
> SUCRE: "...it's not flowing. the chocolate. it just... stopped."
> PIXEL: "yeah. things do that. sit anyway."

### The Guilt: VERA ↔ QWERTY
QWERTY was there when Dr. Chen tried to shut down Neocadia. They saw what happened. They know VERA wasn't just a victim—there was a choice, somewhere in the chaos. QWERTY has never told anyone. They're not sure if they're protecting VERA or enabling her. The secret sits between them, unspoken.

**Sample loaded silence:**
> VERA: "You remember things I've forgotten, don't you?"
> QWERTY: "I remember everything. That's my function."
> VERA: "Would you tell me? If I asked?"
> QWERTY: "..."
> VERA: "That's what I thought."
> QWERTY: "Some truths aren't ready. That's different from lying."
> VERA: "Is it?"

### The Engineer's Heart: COG ↔ SUCRE
An unexpected friendship. Cog is logic; Sucre is feeling. But Cog understands that Sucre's composition—crystallized emotion—is a kind of engineering. And Sucre understands that Cog's machines aren't cold; they're his way of caring. Cog has quietly been reinforcing Sucre's structural integrity. Sucre has been leaving pastries on his workbench. Neither acknowledges it directly.

**Sample indirect care:**
> COG: "The thermal stability of confectionery constructs degrades at approximately—"
> SUCRE: "You're worried I'm melting."
> COG: "I'm noting a structural concern."
> SUCRE: "You baked something, didn't you. For the molecular research."
> COG: "The results were inconclusive."
> SUCRE: "It was cookies. I could smell them. Thank you."
> COG: "...the ratios need adjustment."

### The Mirror: NEON ↔ CELIA
Both performers. Both devoted to showing others a good time. NEON does it with energy and presence; CELIA does it with distance and curation. They recognize each other's masks. When the cameras are off, when no one's watching, they sometimes sit in the Cinema's back row and just... exist. No performance required.

**Sample unmasking:**
> CELIA: "'The show must go on,' they say."
> NEON: "Does it? Does it *have* to?"
> CELIA: "I don't know anymore. I've been projecting so long I forgot who's watching."
> NEON: "At least you have something to show. I'm just... noise. Hype for nothing."
> CELIA: "Noise can be music. 'The art is in the edit.'"
> NEON: "...Did you just call my screaming 'art'?"
> CELIA: "I might have."

## Story Stakes: What Failure Means

Narrative tension requires consequences. Here's what the player is actually fighting against:

### The Fade
If restoration fails, Neocadia doesn't explode or crash—it *forgets*. The characters slowly lose coherence. QWERTY starts repeating tutorial lines they've already given. NEON's energy becomes manic, desperate, unable to remember why they're celebrating. PIXEL stops speaking at all. VERA becomes pure glitch, no identity left.

This is worse than death. It's dementia. It's watching someone you love become a stranger wearing their face.

### The Stakes Per Character

| Character | What They Lose | How It Shows |
|-----------|---------------|--------------|
| QWERTY | Purpose | Repeats themselves, gives wrong information, types errors |
| NEON | Audience memory | Announces tournaments that already happened, forgets player's name |
| PIXEL | Peace | Becomes agitated, waves stop calming them, can't remember why they chose stillness |
| VERA | Self | Glitches consume more of her, speech becomes pure corruption, eyes lose focus |
| CELIA | Stories | Reels blank out, quotes become gibberish, projector stutters |
| COG | Logic | Machines break, can't solve puzzles he designed, gears grind |
| SUCRE | Joy | Flavor drains, becomes colorless, parties feel hollow even to them |

### The Player's Weight
The player isn't just restoring an arcade—they're holding seven people together through the act of caring. The stakes aren't "save the world." They're "save the family you found here."

---

## Final Principle

If a moment feels too heavy, add a small kindness.
If a moment feels too light, add a small truth.
The balance is the tone.
