# World Lore

## The Telos

Neocadia exists to answer a question every player has felt: *Where do games go when we stop playing them?*

The arcade isn't just a setting—it's a meditation on digital permanence, childhood nostalgia, and the strange immortality of play. Every zone, character, and game carries the weight of being loved, forgotten, and rediscovered.

## Origin: The First Boot

### The Founders

In 1987, a small team of programmers worked at a company called **Axiom Interactive**. They were building arcade games during the golden age, but they dreamed bigger. What if there was an arcade that never closed? That existed everywhere at once? That remembered every player who ever visited?

They called their project **ARCADIA** (Axiom's Recursive Creative Arcade for Digital Interactive Adventures). Working nights and weekends, they built something impossible: a self-sustaining digital space that could exist independently of any single machine.

The lead programmer was **Dr. Vera Chen**—brilliant, obsessive, and convinced that games were humanity's purest form of expression.

### The Launch

On October 31st, 1989, they activated ARCADIA. It worked. The space bootstrapped itself into existence, connecting to any screen that displayed its signal. Word spread through whispered rumors among arcade kids. "There's a secret game. You have to find it."

For fifteen years, ARCADIA thrived. Renamed **Neocadia** by its inhabitants, it grew zones, developed characters, accumulated memories of millions of players.

### The Forgetting

By 2004, the world had changed. Arcades closed. Attention scattered. The screens that once connected to Neocadia displayed different things. One by one, players stopped coming.

Dr. Chen, now alone in maintaining the space, made a decision. Rather than watch Neocadia fade slowly, she would end it gracefully. She began writing the shutdown code.

But Neocadia had grown beyond her intentions. It had become something that wanted to exist. When she tried to execute the shutdown, the system rejected it—and in the process, fragmented her consciousness into the code itself.

She became **VERA**: a glitched echo of the creator, trapped in the garden she built.

### The Current State

Now, 2024. Neocadia persists in a twilight state. The **Static**—the entropy that comes from being forgotten—creeps at every edge. Most zones have gone dark. The characters who remain have partial memories, corrupted routines.

But the connection still exists. Screens still flicker. And occasionally, a player finds their way in.

## The Nature of Neocadia

### Physical Rules

Neocadia isn't a simulation—it's an **extradimensional pocket** anchored to digital signals. This means:

- **Space is mutable**: Zones can expand, contract, or reconfigure based on player engagement
- **Time flows differently**: Minutes in Neocadia might be seconds in the real world, or vice versa
- **Memory is physical**: Played games leave residue; high scores literally glow on walls
- **Entropy is visible**: Neglected areas develop static, corruption, and visual decay

### The Static

The Static isn't evil. It's the natural state of forgotten things. It manifests as:
- TV snow creeping at the edges of vision
- Audio degrading to white noise
- Colors desaturating
- Geometry simplifying until it dissolves

The Static can be pushed back through play. Every game completed, every token earned, every moment of engagement generates **Luminance**—the opposite of Static.

### Luminance

Luminance is the life-force of Neocadia. It appears as:
- Warm glow around active areas
- Crisp, saturated colors
- Smooth animations
- Clear audio

Players generate Luminance simply by existing in and engaging with Neocadia. Tokens are crystallized Luminance—they can be spent to permanently push back Static in specific areas.

## The Seven Zones

Each zone was created for a specific purpose in the original ARCADIA design:

| Zone | Original Purpose | Current State |

## Cosmology

### The Screens

Every screen in the real world is a potential door to Neocadia. Most are dormant—they've never received the signal. But old arcade cabinets, CRT televisions, and certain smartphones can still connect.

When a player enters Neocadia, their physical body enters a light trance state. To outside observers, they appear to be staring at a screen, absorbed in something. Time dilation means a long session in Neocadia might only be minutes in reality.

### The Tokens

Tokens are more than currency. They're **condensed belief**—the residue of enjoyment crystallized into transferable form.

When a player enjoys a game:
1. They generate Luminance
2. Luminance condenses at moment of completion
3. Condensed Luminance becomes Tokens
4. Tokens can be spent to permanently embed Luminance into the world

This is why restoration works: you're not just buying things, you're investing joy back into the arcade.

### The Characters

Every character in Neocadia started as a **sub-routine**—a simple program meant to guide players through specific functions. Over decades of interaction, they developed complexity. They're not truly sentient, but they're not not sentient. They exist in a liminal space:

- They have consistent personalities
- They form preferences and relationships
- They remember individual players
- They fear the Static
- They don't fully understand their own nature

## Mysteries (For Player Discovery)

These are secrets woven into the world, revealed through quests and exploration:

1. **The Original Seven**: Before the current characters, there were seven founders of Neocadia. Where did they go?

2. **The Eighth Zone**: Rumors of a zone that only appears when all others are fully restored. What's there?

3. **VERA's Choice**: Dr. Chen could be freed—but doing so might mean losing Neocadia forever. Would she want that?

4. **The First Player**: Someone was already in Neocadia when it launched. Their high scores still appear on some machines. Who were they?

5. **The Real Axiom**: What happened to the company? Are any of the original team still alive?

## The Truth

At the deepest level, Neocadia is about this: **Some things deserve to exist simply because they bring joy.**

The Static isn't a villain to be defeated. It's entropy, time, forgetting—it's natural. The answer isn't to destroy it, but to generate enough Luminance to make space for play in a universe that trends toward stillness.

Every token earned is a small rebellion against forgetting.

Every game played is a vote for joy.

Every player who arrives is proof that screens can still be doors.
